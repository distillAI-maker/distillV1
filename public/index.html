01 · The idea

Greatness,
by taking away.

In 1501, Michelangelo took on a block of marble other sculptors had walked away from. He saw a figure inside it. His work wasn't adding anything; it was taking away everything that wasn't David.

We think a good life works the same way. So much of your day came from a feed, an ad or someone else's routine. Distill helps you clear what was never yours, and keep what makes you feel most like yourself.

Before the story, the distillation

Wanting to be better is good. Somewhere it tipped into more metrics, more products and more protocols, until there was no room left to just live. You're not falling behind. You're carrying too much.

02 · How it works

First, reveal.
Then, shape.

Distill starts where Michelangelo did. Clear the noise first, then shape what's left, one small ritual at a time, into days that feel like yours.

  1. 01Tap everything you do.

    Supplements, classes, memberships, the evening glass of wine. In about a minute you see your whole stack as one number: how many things, and what they cost you each month.

  2. 02Let go, gently.

    What came from TikTok, a friend or an ad and isn't doing anything for you goes, one small thing at a time. Anything that's yours stays yours. The money comes back.

  3. 03Keep what's worth keeping.

    With the noise gone, you build a morning and a night ritual that actually fit, and Distill keeps learning what you're like at your best.

    06

    The human archive

    Instinct, practiced.

    Long before wellness was a category, people built private practices for becoming. Not heroes to copy, but proof: a singular life begins when you take an instinct seriously.

    01 · Florence, 1501

    Michelangelo
    saw the figure.

    Others saw an abandoned block. He trusted what was inside it enough to remove everything that wasn't David.

    02 · Florence, 1418

    Brunelleschi
    saw the curve.

    No one knew how to roof the cathedral. He followed a geometry others couldn't see yet, inventing the tools as he built the dome.

    03 · Milan, c. 1490

    Leonardo
    kept looking.

    Light, water, anatomy, flight. His notebooks made curiosity a daily ritual, and the world got more extraordinary because he never stopped noticing.

    acqua · luce · corpo · moto

    04 · Before dawn, c. 170

    Marcus Aurelius
    wrote to himself.

    Not for an audience, and not for approval. He came back to the page to remember the person he meant to be, before the world made its demands.

    The pattern

    A simple vision. A practice repeated. A story made one choice at a time. History reminds us what a person can become.

    Before you join

    Do I need a wearable?

    No. Distill works without one. If you wear an Oura, an Apple Watch or a Garmin, you can connect it later and Distill will quietly notice what your best nights have in common.

    Will it tell me to give things up?

    Only gently, and only when it's clear something isn't doing anything for you. Anything that's yours, like Friday dinner with your sister, stays yours. You always choose.

    What does it cost?

    Founding membership is free while we build Distill with our first hundred members.

    Is this medical advice?

    No. Distill is wellness, not medicine. It never diagnoses anything, and whatever your doctor gave you stays exactly as it is.
