F

Motion policy

How much moves, and when. Premium sites usually move less than you expect. Reduced-motion preference is respected in all of these.

M1
Still. Nothing animates. Hover is a hard state change. Confidence through stillness. Fits D7, D9, D8.
M2
One opening sequence, then still. The hero assembles once on load over about 1.2 seconds. After that the page does not move. Fits D2, D1.
M3
Scroll-driven counting. Numbers tick down as sections enter. Locked to scroll position so the reader controls it. Fits D3, D9.
M4
Sequential reveals. Strikes, dots, or list items appear one at a time on scroll, about 400ms apart. Fits D5, D6, D8.
M5
Ambient, very slow. A background gradient or line that breathes on an eight-second cycle. Nothing else moves. Fits D6, D12.
M6
Physical. Slips print, stamps land, needles settle. Short, with weight. Fits D11, D10.
M7
Hover-only micro-detail. Rules draw, a second line of a label appears, a number reveals its unit. Rewards attention, invisible otherwise. Fits D1, D4.
M8
One live object. A single continuously rendered thing (the drop, the line, the gauge) and nothing else ever animates. Fits D4, D12, D10.

Surface and material

What the page feels like it is made of. All done with CSS and canvas, no image files.

G1
Flat ink. No texture, no shadow, no gradient. Pure colour and type. The most modern and the hardest to get right.
G2
Paper grain. A faint canvas noise over the ground. Reads as print. Pairs with P5, P8, P11, P17.
G3
Glass. Refraction highlights, soft inner shadows, one light source. Pairs with P4, P16.
G4
Brushed metal. Vertical linear gradients, hairline bezels, a single indicator light. Pairs with P10.
G5
Ruled lines. Faint horizontal rules across the whole page like a notebook or ledger. Pairs with P3, P8.
G6
Visible grid. Hairline column grid drawn on the page. Pairs with P2, P7.

Notes before you choose

On the name. Distill is strong for this product: reduce to what matters, remove the rest, purity through process. Two things to be aware of. First, distilling is also what makes spirits, so any direction with green glass, brass or amber (D1, D3, D10) nudges toward whisky, and Distill's own engine treats alcohol as an observe-only item. Second, "distill" is already a common product name in software, so the visual identity has to do more work than the word.

  • What the current page is. The existing landing page is near-black with a vermilion accent and a serif display. That is the single most common "premium tech" look right now, which is why it feels familiar rather than niche. None of the twelve directions above reuse it.
  • The rule that constrains colour. The product never shows red. The landing page should not either, so every palette here avoids red as an accent. Strike-throughs are blue or graphite.
  • What tends to read premium to this audience. Fewer elements, larger type, more space, real numbers, one idea per screen, and stillness. Gloss, gradients and glow tend to read as a startup.
  • Safest strong picks. D2, D5 and D9 are the most distinctive without leaning on a cliché. D4 and D11 are the biggest swings. D1, D3, D6, D10 are handsome but closest to looks that already exist.
