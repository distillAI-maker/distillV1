Distill Routing Table.

Every item a person might list in their stack, what tier the engine puts it in, why, and the exact sentence they read on day one. Same data as the spreadsheet, which stays the source of truth.

How the engine decides, in eight steps
  1. Protected? From a doctor or blood test, a prescription, a hormone, a clinical service, or for a diagnosed condition. Left alone.
  2. Safety note? Attached to the verdict. A few redirect to Protected.
  3. Goal known? "General health" goes to Tier 3 as untestable, cost shown.
  4. Settled without a test? The row's follow-up question runs. Dose too low, form not absorbed, tested and found nothing, no way it could work, not being used, or overlapping with something else: Tier 2.
  5. Wearable can see it, acts within hours, expected effect at least 0.8 swings? Tier 1. Biggest effect first.
  6. Wearable can see it, but the effect is under 0.8? Tier 3, told plainly, with "run it anyway" offered.
  7. Wearable can see it, but it's slow? Queued for a longer test. Never 3-day blocks.
  8. Everything else is Tier 3, with a daily rating for things you'd feel rather than measure.
