# Distill: direction (9 October 2026)

The founder's call, after the class review ("too much text, too much friction"). Where this and older docs disagree, this wins; raise it with the team rather than building around it.

## What Distill is

A supportive companion that helps a person know themselves and their stack, away from the noise of social media, peers and ads that keep telling them to add more. Wellness is feeling good from the inside and being confident in who you are. Distill takes away what was never theirs, shows them who they are at their best, then helps them become more of it.

Not a lab. No experiments, protocols or verdicts in front of the person.

## The three acts

1. **Clear the noise.** What came from TikTok, a friend or an ad and isn't doing anything for them is let go, gently, one small thing at a time. The money comes back.
2. **Know yourself.** The app reflects back who they are at their best, in their own words and their own data ("Your best nights follow Pilates and early dinners with friends"). Quiet noticing in the background, from the wearable they already wear. The feeling: "I know myself and I'm not behind."
3. **Become more you.** With the noise gone, individual suggestions that fit: a ritual, a class, a place, a trip (a Pilates weekend at Lake Tahoe). Independent, never paid placement; how this earns money is an open decision.

## Onboarding: intake and one moment

Arrival, name, your stack, your ideal day, **the number** (count and monthly cost), join (email, free), connect, then the app. No questions, no sorting, no verdicts during onboarding. The analysis is the app, and it unfolds over days.

## The app

- **Your Standard (home):** a short portrait of them at their best, from their own ideal-day words; the count and the monthly cost going down over time.
- **Today:** one gentle thing a day, about a minute: a question that settles an item, something to let go of, or something we noticed.
- **What we're noticing:** patterns from their nights, said as calm observations with how sure we are, never as a test.
- **The collection:** everything they do, as placards: kept, being read, let go.
- **Made for you (later):** one aligned suggestion at a time.

The engine stays and decides what is said (routing, the estimate-based decision policy from PR #3); the person never sees its machinery. Assigned on/off tests become rare and opt-in.

## How Distill decides (11 October)

Every person has three inputs, ranked:

1. **Their values:** their ideal day in their own words, what they'd never give up, their ritual notes, their daily answers.
2. **Their signals:** their nights, from the wearable.
3. **The evidence:** the routing table.

**Values beat data.** Wine Fridays cost some recovery and are also connection, photos and memories: we celebrate them ("Fridays are yours") and offer a gentle tweak only if they ask. The person is a co-creator throughout: every suggestion is a proposal they shape (keep, let go, not now), and when they say why something is theirs ("it's my thing with my sister"), it is marked theirs and not raised again unless they ask.

- **One thing a day,** rotating: learn about them (one short question, not journaling), let something go, notice a pattern, celebrate a ritual that is clearly theirs, and rarely suggest something. Always skippable, no streaks.
- **Ritual notes:** tap a ritual and write anything, like Apple Notes. We distill the notes into two or three sentences that keep their meaning, in line with the mission (growth, human, no comparison). The model never writes numbers, verdicts or health claims.
- **Their photo:** the top of Your Standard is a moment they love, chosen by them. Until they add one, a quiet painting holds the place.

## Brand

- **Positioning (revised 11 October):** optimization is good: a wearable, a cooling mattress pad, a growth mindset, they make people feel good. The problem is over-optimization: so many metrics, products and protocols that there's no room left to explore and experience life as a human. Distill keeps what works and clears the rest: drop the stress, the comparison and the feeling of not being good enough, and keep everything that makes you feel and look your best. Never anti-Oura or anti-WHOOP; we read their data.
- **Feel:** artistic, editorial, calm, like a great hotel rather than a gym. Not cute: no mascot or avatar; the app speaks like a curator.
- **A museum, not a gym (11 October):** modern life got clean, efficient and minimal, and lost its detail. People are drawn back to the past and to each other (epic films about the ancient world, the return of in-person connection). Distill keeps the detail and craft of classical art and architecture, as a personal mission: art and design should thrive. It shows through the work (a masterpiece on Your Standard each day, captioned like a placard; a line from a Stoic behind it), lightly. Never overdone: no "Know thyself", no Greek words dropped into the copy.
- **Look:** gallery white (#ffffff) with the warm off-white (#f6f5f2) and gold (#b48c37, text #8a6a1f) as secondary, as on the new landing page. Newsreader for titles (a quiet book serif), Switzer for everything else, spaced small caps for labels. Public-domain fine-art imagery (museum open access, CC0): architecture, philosophy and the sublime as well as calm landscapes (`apps/web/lib/art.ts`). No user photo at the top of Your Standard. Items as museum placards: name, what it is, where it came from, status.
- **Voice:** short, calm, certain. One line where we used three. Second person, no exclamation marks, nothing that makes anyone feel behind. Sign-off: "Less noise. More you."
